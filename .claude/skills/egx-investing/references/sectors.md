# EGX Sector Map and Accounting Traps

Every sector on the EGX has a way of looking better or worse than it is. This
file exists so those traps get caught before an analysis is written.

Company figures are **as reported in 2026** and are illustrative of how to read
each sector — they are not current prices or recommendations. Re-verify before
using any number.

## Banks and financials

**Major listed names:** Commercial International Bank (**COMI**) — the largest
private bank and the most liquid stock on the exchange; plus QNB Alahli,
Faisal Islamic Bank, Credit Agricole Egypt, Al Baraka. Non-bank financials
include **EFG Holding (HRHO)**.

**Reference results:** CIB Q2 2026 net profit **EGP 21.49bn, +28.6% YoY**;
H1 2026 **EGP 39.3bn, +18%**. Net interest income +20% to EGP 31.1bn,
non-interest income **+71%** to EGP 3.2bn. Loans EGP 597.7bn, deposits
**EGP 1.3tn**.

### The trap: high rates flatter banks, and rates are cyclical

Egyptian banks are enormously profitable when the CBE holds rates at 19–20%.
They borrow from depositors cheaply relative to what they earn on government
paper and lending. **Net interest margin is a policy variable, not a
management achievement.**

So the questions that actually matter:

1. **How much of profit is net interest income vs. fees?** Fee and
   non-interest income is the durable part. CIB's non-interest income growing
   **+71%** matters more than the headline, because it survives rate cuts.
2. **What happens when the CBE cuts?** Egypt began cutting in February 2026.
   A bank whose earnings are pure NIM will see them compress as the cycle turns.
3. **Loan-to-deposit ratio.** CIB at roughly EGP 598bn loans against EGP 1.3tn
   deposits is around 46% — meaning a large share sits in government
   securities. That is *safe* but also means the bank is effectively a
   leveraged bet on Egyptian sovereign paper.
4. **Asset quality.** NPL ratios and coverage. High inflation flatters
   nominal loan books while quietly stressing borrowers' ability to service.

**Sovereign linkage is the real risk.** A large Egyptian bank holding heavy
government debt is not independent of the Egyptian state's fiscal position.
Bank equity and sovereign risk are the same trade wearing two hats.

## Real estate

**Major listed names:** **Talaat Moustafa Group (TMGH)**, **Palm Hills (PHDC)**,
SODIC, Heliopolis Housing, Madinet Masr, Emaar Misr, Orascom Development.

**Reference results:** TMG H1 2026 contracted sales **EGP 219.1bn** (~$4.47bn),
the highest half-year in the company's history; "The Spine" project alone
~EGP 34bn. But Q1 2026 revenue was EGP 13.1bn (+9%) and net profit EGP 5.5bn
(**+9%**). Unrecognized (backlog) sales **EGP 457.9bn** at 31 March 2026.
Palm Hills Q1 2026 net profit EGP 1.205bn, **−21.6% YoY despite revenue
growth**.

### The trap: contracted sales are not revenue

This is the single biggest source of bad Egyptian real estate analysis.

Egyptian developers sell off-plan on **8–10 year installment plans**. Revenue
is recognized on **delivery**, not on sale. So:

- **Contracted sales** = what was sold this period (a demand signal, and a
  *future* revenue indicator)
- **Recognized revenue** = deliveries from units sold years ago
- **Backlog / unrecognized sales** = the bridge between them

TMG selling EGP 219bn while reporting 9% profit growth is not a contradiction —
it is the model working exactly as designed. **Analyze the backlog for
visibility, not the quarter's profit.**

### The inflation problem nobody prices properly

Those installments are **fixed nominal EGP amounts collected over a decade**.
At 16–17% inflation, an installment due in 2034 is worth a fraction of its
face value in today's purchasing power. Developers with long collection
periods and fixed pricing are **structurally short the currency**.

Mitigants to check: does the developer index installments? What are the down
payment percentages? Shorter plans and larger down payments materially reduce
this exposure.

### Reading Palm Hills correctly

**Revenue up, profit down −21.6%** is a margin story: construction costs
(steel, cement, labor) rising faster than the company can raise prices.
In an inflationary economy this is a **structural weakness signal**, not a soft
quarter — it says the company lacks pricing power. Treat it as a red flag.

**Checklist for any Egyptian developer:** backlog size and trend, collection
period, down payment %, land bank cost basis (old land bought pre-devaluation
is a hidden asset), net debt, and margin direction.

## Fertilizers and chemicals

**Major listed names:** **Abu Qir Fertilizers (ABUK)**, MOPCO, Sidi Kerir
Petrochemicals (SKPC), Egyptian Chemical Industries (Kima).

**Reference results:** Abu Qir H1 2026 net profit **EGP 10.01bn vs 4.57bn
(+119%)**; revenue EGP 23.52bn, **+86%**. Driven by stable gas supply and
exports.

### Why this sector deserves attention

This is the clearest **natural hedge** on the EGX, and the mechanism is worth
understanding precisely:

- **Revenue in USD** (exports, globally priced commodity)
- **Costs in EGP** (domestic natural gas feedstock, local labor)

So EGP devaluation **expands margins**. And because global fertilizer prices
track energy prices, an energy crisis that hurts almost every other Egyptian
company *helps* this one. Abu Qir's +119% during a global energy shock is that
mechanism in action.

### The trap: gas supply and pricing are political

The entire margin structure depends on the government continuing to supply
natural gas at favorable domestic prices. That is a **policy decision, revisable
at any time**. Egypt has repeatedly adjusted industrial gas pricing under
fiscal pressure, and periodic supply curtailments have hit production.

So this sector is a bet on two things: global commodity prices *and* Egyptian
gas policy. The second is the one that gets ignored.

Also note these are **cyclical commodity earnings**. A P/E of 5 on peak-cycle
earnings is not cheap — it is a warning. Check the price of the underlying
commodity against its own history before treating a low multiple as value.

## Industrials and building materials

**Major listed names:** **Elsewedy Electric (SWDY)**, **Ezz Steel (ESRS)**,
Egyptian Iron & Steel, cement producers (Suez Cement, Misr Beni Suef),
Egyptian Aluminum.

**Reference results:** Elsewedy Q1 2026 net profit EGP 4.85bn (**+16.9%**);
revenue **EGP 75.3bn, +26.8%**.

### What to check

**Elsewedy** is a large-cap industrial with significant regional and
international project exposure — genuine hard-currency revenue, but also
project execution risk and lumpy earnings. Note the growth pattern: revenue
+26.8% against profit +16.9% means **margin compression**. Worth investigating,
not celebrating.

**Steel and cement** are energy-intensive and domestically oriented. They are
squeezed from both ends: energy costs rise with the global shock, while
construction demand depends on a domestic economy under fiscal pressure.
Import competition and tariff policy also swing results.

For all of these: **check whether inputs are imported**. A manufacturer buying
raw materials in USD and selling in EGP is the most currency-exposed profile
on the exchange.

## Consumer staples

**Major listed names:** **Juhayna (JUFO)**, Edita, Obour Land, Domty, Cairo
Poultry, Delta Sugar.

**Reference results:** Juhayna H1 2026 net profit **EGP 1.38bn, +77%**;
net sales EGP 17.67bn (from EGP 14.16bn).

### The pricing-power test

Consumer staples in Egypt are a clean natural experiment in pricing power.
Everyone's input costs rose. The question is who could pass them on.

Juhayna's profit (+77%) growing far faster than sales (+25%) means **margins
expanded** — the company raised prices faster than costs rose and volumes
held. That is real pricing power and the single most valuable trait a company
can have in a high-inflation economy.

Compare to Palm Hills (revenue up, profit down) to see the contrast clearly.

**What to check:** margin direction, volume vs. price contribution to growth
(price-driven growth with falling volumes is fragile), input import exposure
(packaging, wheat, and feed are often dollar-denominated), and how far
consumers can be pushed before they trade down to cheaper brands.

## Telecom, tech and fintech

**Major listed names:** **Telecom Egypt (ETEL)**, **Fawry (FWRY)**,
**e-finance (EFIH)**, MM Group.

**Reference results:** e-finance Q1 2026 net profit **EGP 856.9m, +42.3%**;
revenue +38.8%.

### What to check

**Telecom Egypt** carries heavy capex and USD-denominated equipment costs
against EGP revenue — a classic devaluation-exposed profile, partly offset by
international wholesale/transit revenue in hard currency. Check the FX
translation impact on debt.

**Fintech (Fawry, e-finance)** rides genuine structural growth: financial
inclusion, digitization of government payments, and a young population. These
trade at **much higher multiples** than the rest of the market. The question is
whether growth justifies the premium — apply a growth-adjusted comparison
rather than a raw P/E, and check whether growth is transaction volume or just
inflation flowing through fee-based revenue.

**e-finance specifically** has heavy government-contract exposure. That is both
a moat and a concentration risk.

## Tobacco

**Major listed name:** **Eastern Company (EAST)** — a near-monopoly in Egyptian
tobacco.

**Reference results:** Full-year net profit **EGP 9.71bn, +6%**; sales
EGP 37.38bn (from EGP 20.44bn). Dividend of **EGP 1.45/share** approved by the
general assembly, against a board proposal of EGP 2.85.

### What to check

The classic defensive: inelastic demand, dominant market position, strong cash
generation, and a reliable dividend. In an inflationary economy that dividend
is a large part of the total return case.

But look at the gap between sales (+83%) and profit (+6%). That gap is
**excise tax**. Tobacco is a favored target for fiscal consolidation, and Egypt
has raised tobacco taxes repeatedly. **The government is effectively a senior
claimant on this company's revenue**, and its take can rise by decree.

Also note the dividend gap: the board proposed EGP 2.85 and the assembly
approved EGP 1.45. Proposed dividends are not declared dividends — never model
income off a board proposal.

## Healthcare and pharma

**Major listed names:** Cleopatra Hospitals, Ibnsina Pharma, EIPICO, Rameda.

Led sector performance in early August 2026 (+8.9% in a week).

**What to check:** pharma manufacturers import most active ingredients in USD
while selling into a **price-regulated** domestic market — a structurally
squeezed position where devaluation cannot be passed on freely. Hospital
operators have more pricing freedom. Distributors run on thin margins and high
volumes, so working capital is the thing to watch.

## Tourism, transport and Suez-linked

**Major listed names:** Orascom Development Egypt, Egyptian Transport (Egytrans),
Alexandria Container Handling, various logistics names.

**The 2026-specific issue:** the Red Sea / Hormuz disruption directly reduces
Suez Canal transit revenue, hits port and container volumes, and weighs on
tourism sentiment. Companies in this cluster are **direct plays on regional
conflict resolution**, whether or not their management says so.

They rally hardest on de-escalation news and fall hardest on escalation. If a
portfolio holds these alongside fertilizer exporters, those two positions are
partially hedging each other — which is worth pointing out, since investors
often hold both without realizing they've offset their own bet.

## Cross-sector: the currency lens

Sector labels matter less than this classification. Before anything else, put
the company in one of three buckets:

| Bucket | Profile | Devaluation impact | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| **USD earner** | Revenue in hard currency, costs in EGP | **Tailwind** — margins expand | Fertilizer exporters, some Elsewedy revenue, tourism inflows |
| **Domestic EGP** | Both sides in EGP | Neutral locally, **dilutive in USD terms** | Banks, local retail, domestic real estate |
| **USD cost base** | Costs in hard currency, revenue in EGP | **Direct margin hit** | Pharma manufacturers, importers, capex-heavy telecom |

With models pointing toward EGP 52–55 per USD against roughly 46.8 in August
2026, this classification is not academic. **A portfolio holding only bucket
two and three is short the Egyptian pound without having chosen to be.**

Note the important nuance in bucket two: a bank can post excellent EGP results
and still destroy dollar wealth. That is not a criticism of the bank — it is a
reason the portfolio needs bucket-one exposure to balance it.
